CENTRAL OBSERVATION
“aNUma does not have a meaning problem. It has a translation and delivery problem.”

Across the fieldwork, respondents describe potent experiences, scientific evidence, committed collaborators and genuine demand. What is missing is a legible path from research-led potential to responsible, sustainable access.

Boardroom synthesis

01 / CHALLENGESWhat blocks movement now
  • Hardware is not a detail.

    All seven respondents name the headset, VR perception or access as a barrier.

  • The explanation cost is too high.

    Audience, practice, science, spirituality, company and nonprofit compete before the value is understood.

  • Commercial confidence is thin.

    The research reputation has matured faster than a repeatable offer, channel or pricing logic.

02 / OPPORTUNITIESWhat can become an advantage
  • Own the practice, not the headset.

    “Evidence-informed immersive practice” can travel across devices and contexts.

  • Turn proof into a trust journey.

    Research earns credibility; testimony and guided trial convert curiosity into felt understanding.

  • Use facilitators as the first channel.

    They add context, distribution, continuity and a route from solo interest to shared depth.

03 / BLINDSPOTSWhat is not yet sufficiently designed
  • Potency has product consequences.

    A profound experience needs eligibility, exit, escalation and integration protocols—not only beautiful onboarding.

  • A broad vision still needs a wedge.

    “Many possible applications” can postpone the hard choice of who pays first and why.

  • Identity cannot outrun operations.

    A new look without a new doorway, product continuity and ownership model will reproduce the old confusion.

Progress diagnosis

The work succeeded as research before it succeeded as a market offer.

This is not a verdict on the experience. It is a distinction between three kinds of progress that have moved at different speeds.

WHAT WORKED

A distinctive experiential and research core

The team has created a recognisable aesthetic, meaningful group encounters, peer-reviewed inquiry and a network of committed collaborators.

  • Original numadelic embodiment
  • Credibility through published research
  • Strong conviction after direct experience
WHAT STALLED

The path from interest to participation

Potential participants must decode an unfamiliar category, trust VR, obtain equipment, understand the benefit and find a session before they can discover the value.

  • High explanation cost
  • Hardware and distribution friction
  • No repeatable paid doorway yet
WHAT CHANGED

The strategy can now separate practice from device

The emerging opportunity is to treat VR as the highest-fidelity expression of a broader practice system rather than the definition of the brand.

  • More formats can carry the aesthetic principles
  • Facilitators can become a trusted channel
  • The app can connect preparation and continuity

Commercial signal

The team aligns on testing the model—not pretending the price is settled.

Five of the six respondents who answered the facilitator-pricing question chose “we need to test the model.” The only specific model selected was an ongoing professional subscription.

STRONGEST ALIGNMENT

Pricing should be learned through use

The fieldwork does not support one universal price or one customer model. Participant, facilitator, institutional and creator value should be tested separately.

  • 5 of 6 answered “test the model”
  • Avoid premature pricing architecture
  • Compare willingness to pay with delivery cost
FIRST HYPOTHESIS

Professional continuity may support subscription

A subscription becomes credible when facilitators receive recurring value: training, trusted profiles, session tools, practice materials, bookings and an active community of practice.

  • Sell an operating relationship, not software access
  • Test a founding facilitator cohort first
  • Measure bookings, use and renewal intent
MONETISATION LOGIC

Charge for the container around the experience

The most defensible value lies in readiness, guidance, continuity and governed delivery—not in selling intensity or making unsupported promises.

  • Participants pay for prepared pathways
  • Practitioners pay for tools and reach
  • Institutions pay for governed implementation

Positioning / working draft

A clear sentence before a clever slogan

For contemplatively curious people and the practitioners who guide them, aNUma is an evidence-informed immersive practice platform that creates carefully prepared conditions for exploring perspective, embodiment and relation—solo and together. Unlike entertainment-first VR or passive meditation apps, it combines a weakly representational numadelic aesthetic, responsible preparation and scientific inquiry.
CLAIM GUARDRAIL

Describe the conditions aNUma designs and the outcomes research has observed. Never prescribe a participant’s meaning, guarantee connection or transformation, promise treatment, or claim equivalence to psychedelics.

Brand identity system

One idea, expressed as story, ethos, style and behaviour.

The identity should translate the numadelic aesthetic into every encounter without turning the research into a decorative cosmic theme.

BRAND STORY

We understand more than we can always embody.

aNUma begins in the gap between concepts and lived experience. It offers carefully prepared encounters that may loosen familiar representations and invite participants to discover their own meaning.

  • Start with a recognisable human gap
  • Introduce the practice before the technology
  • End with reflection and a next step
BRAND ETHOS

Design conditions, not conclusions.

The practice makes space for emergence. The brand should be equally humble: invitational, participant-led and precise about what is observed versus what is promised.

  • Possibility before prescription
  • Care before scale
  • Evidence as inquiry, not authority theatre
BRAND STYLE

Luminous, diffuse and open to interpretation.

Use soft boundaries, restrained radiance, emergence and coalescence. Avoid photorealism, spiritual shorthand and symbols that collapse the experience into one fixed meaning.

  • Low structural specificity
  • Low symbolic rigidity
  • Warm darkness and meaningful motion
BRAND BEHAVIOUR

Make every doorway feel prepared.

Website, app, headset and facilitated session should choreograph clarity, readiness, consent, encounter and integration as one continuous practice.

  • Explain only what helps entry
  • Make risk and agency visible
  • Always offer a way to continue

Five operating principles

The brand must behave before it decorates

01

Possibility over prescription

Create space for participants to construct meaning. Do not visually or verbally tell them what they are supposed to discover.

02

Invitation over prescription

Create curiosity and agency. Avoid preaching, dogma and claims about what someone should experience.

03

Evidence without sterility

Make the science visible and legible while protecting warmth, poetry and first-person testimony.

04

Context is part of the product

Preparation, consent, facilitation and integration are not support content; they are core experience design.

05

Technology serves attention

Use VR only where immersion, embodiment or remote co-presence materially improves the practice.

Method and limits

01

Findings describe repeated or divergent response patterns. Closed-choice counts are calculated directly from the seven responses.

02

Interpretations are reasoned hypotheses about what those patterns mean. They are deliberately labelled and should be challenged.

03

Recommendations apply strategic judgment. They are not automatically produced by majority vote.

This is a small internal sample, not market research. Qualitative strength reflects recurrence, specificity and relevance—not statistical significance.
