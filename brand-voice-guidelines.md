## Executive Summary

aNUma sounds like someone who has been somewhere unusual and can describe it plainly. The team agrees, with rare consistency, on what the voice must never be: dogmatic, elitist, preachy, corporate, trendy (each rejected by 3 or 4 of 6). They also agree on what it must never promise: healing, cure, guaranteed transformation, or equivalence with psychedelics.

The unresolved risk is the opposite one. Half the team (3 of 6) named **"becoming safe and forgettable"** as the most damaging failure for the next identity, and the slider vote leans 73% toward *provocative* over *reassuring*. The current draft language leans hard on caution ("carefully prepared", "responsible", "conditions") and says little about what the experience actually is. The voice needs to be **bold in what it invites, exact in what it claims.**

The single most useful idea in the fieldwork came from Valerie: people *know* contemplative ideas but do not *feel* them. aNUma's voice lives in that gap: concrete about the experience (you see yourself and others as light), honest about the evidence, and generous with the reader's own meaning.

## We Are / We Are Not

| We Are | We Are Not |
|--------|------------|
| **Lucid.** We say what it is in the first sentence. | **Reductive.** We never shrink it to "a VR wellness app". |
| **Wonder-filled.** We let the experience sound as strange and beautiful as it is. | **Vague.** No cosmic filler, no "unlock your potential". |
| **Invitational.** We open a "what if? why not?" window. | **Preachy or dogmatic.** We never tell people what they will discover. |
| **Evidence-informed.** We cite what was measured, with its limits. | **Clinical or overclaiming.** No cure, no treat, no guarantees. |
| **Warm and embodied.** We write to a body in a room, not a user in a funnel. | **Corporate or cold.** No platform-speak, no growth jargon. |
| **Provocative.** We are willing to challenge how VR, meditation and attention are usually sold. | **Safe and forgettable.** A hedge in every sentence is its own failure. |
| **Humble.** We say "we don't know yet" when we don't. | **Timid.** Humility is about claims, not about conviction. |
| **Open to all who are ready.** Readiness, not status, is the entry requirement. | **Elitist.** Not a luxury retreat product, not an insiders' club. |

### Voice Attributes Detail

#### 1. Lucid, not reductive
- **What it means:** The first sentence explains; the second can wonder. Plain nouns before abstractions.
- **How it shows up:** Name the concrete thing ("you and others appear as light") before the category ("immersive practice"). One idea per sentence.
- **Avoid:** Stacking abstractions ("conditions for shifts in felt perspective, meaning and relation").
- **Evidence:** Joe: "We need to sharpen the quick, short message to something that piques interest without being confusing." Catherine: "be much more specific and clear." 2 of 6 name "remaining abstract and hard to explain" as the top risk.
- **Confidence:** High

#### 2. Wonder-filled, not vague
- **What it means:** The poetry comes from precise images, not from big words.
- **How it shows up:** Use sensory, specific images the team already uses: light, coalescence, boundlessness, "sensory underwhelm", "less is more".
- **Avoid:** "Transformative", "consciousness journey", "unlock", "elevate".
- **Evidence:** Poetic/literal slider mean 40 (60% poetic). David: "Beyond this material appearance, we are all boundless fields of energy." "Vague" rejected by 2 of 6.
- **Confidence:** High

#### 3. Invitational, not preachy
- **What it means:** We invite curiosity; we do not persuade or convert.
- **How it shows up:** Questions, second person, low-pressure calls to action ("See for yourself").
- **Avoid:** Imperatives about inner life ("Awaken", "Heal", "Find your true self").
- **Evidence:** Gregory: "invite people in to be curious, not trying to make them." Valerie: "we're not trying to convince them, we just want to generate enough curiosity that they're willing to give it a go." Dogmatic 4/6, Preachy 3/6 rejected.
- **Confidence:** High

#### 4. Evidence-informed, not clinical
- **What it means:** Science earns entry; testimony makes it felt. Both, every time.
- **How it shows up:** "In studies, participants reported..." rather than "aNUma reduces...". Pair every data point with a human voice.
- **Avoid:** "Proven", "clinically shown", "treats", "therapy" (outside governed clinical contexts).
- **Evidence:** Catherine: "do not promise things that have not first been measured." All 5 respondents who answered the never-claim question named cure/heal/equivalence claims.
- **Confidence:** High

#### 5. Warm and embodied, not corporate
- **What it means:** Bodies, breath, rooms, people. Not users, funnels, content.
- **How it shows up:** "Participants", "people", "practice", "session". Short sentences that can be read aloud by a facilitator.
- **Avoid:** "Users", "content library", "engagement", "scale" in public copy.
- **Evidence:** Embodied 3/6, Warm 2/6, Restorative 3/6 chosen; Corporate 3/6, Cold 2/6 rejected.
- **Confidence:** Medium-High

#### 6. Provocative, not preachy
- **What it means:** We can challenge the category (VR that "extracts attention", apps built to be consumed) without lecturing the reader.
- **How it shows up:** Contrasts that surprise: "VR built to quiet you down." "Less to look at. More to notice."
- **Avoid:** Attacking other practices, psychedelics or traditions. Valerie: never claim "we are better than traditional practices".
- **Evidence:** Reassuring/provocative slider mean 73. 3/6 fear "safe and forgettable". Gregory: "VR that doesn't suck"; "using VR to nurture attention rather than extract it".
- **Confidence:** Medium (strong signal, but provocation has not been tested with outside audiences)

## Brand Personality

- **Working archetype:** The Intimate Explorer (Lover leads, Explorer supplies motion, Mystic supplies wonder, with Mystic's shadow of over-promising named by 4/6 as a real risk). Internal tool only, not public language.
- **If aNUma were a person:** A meditation teacher who also runs a research lab. Speaks softly, laughs easily, cites the paper when asked, and would rather you try it than take their word for it.
- **Values expressed in voice:** Sacredness of life (Gregory), humility about evidence (Catherine), altruism and honouring wisdom (Justin), intention to help, not entertain (Joe), less is more (Valerie), the aesthetic as the core (David).

## Messaging Framework

### Primary Value Proposition (draft, for team choice)
**From knowing to feeling.** aNUma creates guided immersive practices where you, and the people with you, appear as light, so contemplative ideas you already know can become something you feel.

Variations observed in the fieldwork:
- "We design immersive experiences that reveal what words alone can't touch." (Valerie)
- "Beyond this material appearance, we are all boundless fields of energy." (David)
- "People deserve connection." (Joe)
- "aNUma exists to nurture that which is sacred about human attention." (Gregory)

### Key Message Pillars

1. **Knowing to feeling (the gap)**
   - Core idea: Many people understand contemplative ideas but have never felt them.
   - When to use: First screen, first 10 seconds, social, talks.
   - Example: "You've read about boundlessness. This is a chance to feel it."
   - Source strength: Valerie (verbatim "knowing but not feeling"), echoed by David and Justin.

2. **Light, not likeness (the aesthetic)**
   - Core idea: Instead of realistic avatars, people appear as soft fields of light. Less detail leaves more room for your own meaning.
   - When to use: Explaining what makes aNUma different from other VR.
   - Example: "No realistic avatars. No busy worlds. Just light, breath and the people with you."
   - Source strength: David (core of aNUma), Valerie (less is more), Gregory (sensory underwhelm), Frontiers 2024 paper.

3. **Studied in the open (credibility)**
   - Core idea: Peer-reviewed research and participant voices, side by side, with honest limits.
   - When to use: After the invitation; partner and press materials.
   - Example: "Published research. Real participants. And a clear line on what we don't yet know."
   - Source strength: all six respondents name science and testimony as proof.

4. **Start alone, continue together (continuity)**
   - Core idea: A prepared solo session is a way in; group practice and community are where it deepens.
   - When to use: Product pages, app, facilitator materials.
   - Example: "Begin with a guided solo session. Then find others who've been there too."
   - Source strength: David, Joe, Justin describe this path explicitly; Gregory and Catherine emphasise the group as the heart.

### Competitive Positioning
- **vs. doing nothing** (Joe: "overwhelmingly, the competition is not doing anything at all"): lower the effort and fear of the first try.
- **vs. meditation apps (Headspace, Calm):** those guide you with words; aNUma lets you *feel* the state the words describe. Do not disparage them.
- **vs. entertainment VR:** attention-nurturing, not attention-extracting. Minimal by design.
- **vs. psychedelics / breathwork retreats:** never claim equivalence. Position as a distinct, non-pharmacological practice informed by the same research traditions.

## Tone-by-Context Matrix

| Context | Formality | Energy | Technical depth | Key principle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Website first screen | Low-Medium | Medium-High | Low | Concrete image, one invitation, one action |
| Social / discovery | Low | High | Low | Curiosity hook, never a claim |
| Pre-session preparation & safety (app) | Medium | Calm | Medium | Clear, kind, direct. Safety copy is never poetic |
| In-headset / facilitation | Low | Very calm | None | Short, spoken, present tense, lots of silence |
| Integration & community | Low | Warm | Low | Reflective questions, no interpretation of their experience |
| Research, partners, grants | High | Medium | High | Precise citations, stated limits |
| Facilitator training | Medium | Medium | Medium-High | Practical, peer-to-peer, standards stated plainly |
| Internal strategy (this page) | Medium | Medium-High | Medium | Candid, specific, attributed. Name the hard truths |

### Context notes
- **Safety and consent copy:** the one place where poetry is off. Say who should not take part, what can happen, and how to leave the experience, in plain words. (David and Valerie both flagged this; Valerie specifically raised risks like surfacing difficult memories.)
- **In-headset:** read every line aloud before shipping. If it cannot be spoken slowly by a facilitator, rewrite it.
- **Internal strategy:** the core team fears being forgettable more than being misunderstood. Internal documents should model directness: name decisions, name disagreements, attribute quotes.

## Terminology Guide

### Must-use
| Term | Usage | Instead of |
|---|---|---|
| **aNUma** | Always this casing in written copy | Anuma, ANUMA, anuma |
| **numadelic** | Lowercase; on first use pair with a plain definition ("numadelic: an aesthetic where people appear as light") | Unexplained jargon |
| **Numadelic Labs** | Full name for the nonprofit research organisation | NL, NLC in public copy |
| **participant** | People taking part in an experience | user, patient (outside clinical settings), customer |
| **practice / session / experience** | Practice = the ongoing thing; session = one occurrence; experience = a specific designed piece (Boundless, Clear Light) | content, product, module |
| **evidence-informed** | Describing the approach | evidence-based, proven, clinically validated |

### Avoid
| Term | Why | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| "reconnecting humans through VR" | Valerie finds it aversive; frames the future of connection as tech-mediated | "Feel connected to the people with you" |
| "digital psychedelic", "trip" | Equivalence claim; legal and ethical risk | "a non-pharmacological practice" |
| "transformative", "unlock", "awaken" | Vague, promise-shaped | Describe the concrete experience |
| "platform" (public copy) | Corporate; also contested internally (is aNUma a company or a design platform?) | "practice", "experiences" |
| "users", "content library", "engagement" | Corporate, consumption framing | participants, experiences, practice |
| "doorway" (overused in the draft, 14 times) | Loses force through repetition | "way in", "first step", or the specific action |

### Never use
| Claim | Reason |
|---|---|
| heal / cure / treat anxiety, depression or any condition | Named by the team as a claim the brand must never make |
| "completely safe" / "no side effects" | Valerie and David: risks exist and must be stated |
| "same as psychedelics" / "better than meditation" | Equivalence and superiority claims |
| "a complete spiritual path" | Justin: never claim this |

## Content Examples

**Before (current draft):** "aNUma is an evidence-informed immersive practice platform that creates carefully prepared conditions for exploring perspective, embodiment and relation."
**After:** "aNUma is a guided practice in immersive light. You and the people with you appear as soft fields of light, so ideas you've only read about, like boundlessness or connection, can become something you feel."
**Why:** Concrete image first; no stacked abstractions; the promise is an invitation, not an outcome.

**Before:** "A carefully prepared invitation into an experience whose meaning remains yours."
**After:** "We prepare the way in. The meaning is yours."
**Why:** Same idea, half the words, sayable out loud.

**Before (safety):** "Make risk and agency visible."
**After (in-app):** "This experience can bring up strong feelings. If it gets too much, lift the headset or say 'pause'. You can stop at any time."
**Why:** Safety copy must be literal and actionable.

## Open Questions for Team Discussion

### High priority
1. **Option A or B: "From knowing to feeling" or "From knowing to being"?**
   - Found: Both derive from Valerie's "knowing but not feeling".
   - Recommendation: **A for public copy** (concrete, emotionally legible), B as an internal ethos line.
   - Need from you: Room vote, then a 5-person comprehension test with outsiders.

2. **Is aNUma a company, a design platform, or a public brand?**
   - Found: Four favour "one brand, two expressions", but Justin writes that the company "should end and aNUma should refer to the design platform", and Valerie understood the plan was to move to a nonprofit-led dual structure.
   - Recommendation: Resolve this before any naming or voice rollout. Voice guidance holds either way; *who speaks* changes.
   - Need from you: A leadership decision in the first 0-6 week window.

3. **How much sacred language is allowed?**
   - Found: Catherine asks to "move away from spiritual ideas"; Justin and Gregory centre the sacred.
   - Recommendation: Science-first sentence, sacred second. Allow "contemplative", "wonder", "the sacred" in depth content; keep the first screen secular.
   - Need from you: Confirm or override.

### Medium priority
4. **Public use of "numadelic".** Recommendation: keep it (it is ownable), but always pair it with a plain definition until comprehension tests say otherwise.
5. **Spanish voice.** The team operates in at least two languages (David). Recommendation: create a bilingual glossary so "participant", "practice", "integration" etc. translate consistently; reuse the approach from the Clear Light Spanish glossary.
6. **Named proof points.** Media mentions and partnerships listed by the team (e.g. MIT Technology Review, El País, the Preparing to Die programme) should be verified and cleared before public use.
