Core-team fieldwork / evidence-led draft

Everyone who tries it gets it.
Almost no one gets to try it.

Six people who built aNUma answered 70 questions each. This page keeps three things apart: what you said, what it might mean, and what the brand could do next. Where you agree, that is evidence. Where you disagree, that is design material. Nothing here is decided by vote.

01 / TEAM FINDINGS

What the responses actually say

You agree on why.
You disagree on how.

On the value and ethics of the work, your six answers are strikingly close. They split when that value has to become a first audience, an offer, a business model and a story a stranger can understand in ten seconds.

CENTRAL OBSERVATION
“aNUma doesn’t have a meaning problem. It has a translation and delivery problem.”

Across the fieldwork: potent experiences, published science, committed collaborators and people asking to take part. What is missing is a clear, safe and sustainable way for someone outside the lab to get in, and to keep going.

Honest read

The opportunity is real. The route isn’t built yet.

What you name again and again, set beside what you may be underestimating.

01 / CHALLENGESWhat blocks movement now
  • Hardware is not a detail.

    All six respondents name the headset, VR perception or access as a barrier.

  • The explanation cost is too high.

    Before anyone feels the value, they have to sort through practice, science, spirituality, a company and a nonprofit.

  • Commercial confidence is thin.

    The research reputation has matured faster than a repeatable offer, channel or pricing logic.

02 / OPPORTUNITIESWhat can become an advantage
  • Own the practice, not the headset.

    “Evidence-informed immersive practice” can travel across devices and contexts.

  • Let proof and testimony work together.

    Research earns trust. Hearing from participants, and trying it with a guide, turns curiosity into understanding.

  • Use facilitators as the first channel.

    They add context, distribution, continuity and a route from solo interest to shared depth.

03 / BLINDSPOTSWhat is not yet sufficiently designed
  • Potency has product consequences.

    A profound experience needs clear rules for who takes part, how to leave, what to do when it goes wrong and how to integrate it. Valerie notes the team has no therapist’s perspective yet, and may underestimate the risks.

  • A broad vision still needs a wedge.

    “Many possible applications” can postpone the hard choice of who pays first and why.

  • Identity cannot outrun operations.

    A new look without a new way in, a joined-up product and a clear owner will reproduce the old confusion.

Progress diagnosis

The work succeeded as research before it succeeded as a market offer.

This is not a verdict on the experience. It is a distinction between three kinds of progress that have moved at different speeds.

WHAT WORKED

A distinctive experiential and research core

The team has created a recognisable aesthetic, meaningful group encounters, peer-reviewed inquiry and a network of committed collaborators.

  • Original numadelic embodiment
  • Credibility through published research
  • Strong conviction after direct experience
WHAT STALLED

The path from interest to participation

Before anyone feels the value, they must decode an unfamiliar category, trust VR, find a headset, understand the benefit and find a session. As one of you put it: “few people who were our target audience actually want to put on a VR headset.”

  • High explanation cost
  • Hardware and distribution friction
  • No repeatable paid offer yet
WHAT CHANGED

The strategy can now separate practice from device

The emerging opportunity is to treat VR as the highest-fidelity expression of a broader practice system rather than the definition of the brand.

  • More formats can carry the aesthetic principles
  • Facilitators can become a trusted channel
  • The app can connect preparation and continuity

Access is the product problem

The headset is one of six places people drop out.

Nobody can value an experience they can’t find, understand, get to, enter safely or continue.

Positioning / working draft

A clear sentence before a clever slogan

For people drawn to contemplative practice, and the guides who teach it, aNUma is an evidence-informed immersive practice: you and the people with you appear as soft fields of light, so ideas you have only read about can become something you feel, alone or together. Unlike VR built to entertain or apps built to be consumed, aNUma is minimal by design, prepared with care and studied in the open.
CLAIM GUARDRAIL

Describe the conditions aNUma designs and the outcomes research has observed. Never prescribe a participant’s meaning, guarantee connection or transformation, promise treatment, or claim equivalence to psychedelics.

Brand identity system

One idea, expressed as story, ethos, style and behaviour.

The identity should translate the numadelic aesthetic into every encounter without turning the research into a decorative cosmic theme.

BRAND STORY

We know more than we have ever felt.

aNUma starts in the gap between understanding an idea and living it. It offers prepared experiences that loosen familiar ways of seeing, and leaves the meaning to you.

  • Start with a recognisable human gap
  • Introduce the practice before the technology
  • End with reflection and a next step
BRAND ETHOS

Design conditions, not conclusions.

The practice makes space for emergence. The brand should be equally humble: invitational, participant-led and precise about what is observed versus what is promised.

  • Curiosity before conviction
  • Care before scale
  • Evidence as inquiry, not as a badge
BRAND STYLE

Luminous, diffuse and open to interpretation.

Use soft boundaries, restrained radiance, emergence and coalescence. Avoid photorealism, spiritual shorthand and symbols that collapse the experience into one fixed meaning.

  • Low structural specificity
  • Low symbolic rigidity
  • Warm darkness and meaningful motion
BRAND BEHAVIOUR

Every first step should feel held.

Website, app, headset and facilitator should carry a person through the same five beats: clarity, readiness, consent, encounter, integration.

  • Explain only what helps entry
  • Make risk and agency visible
  • Always offer a way to continue

Five operating principles

The brand must behave before it decorates

01

The meaning belongs to the participant

Leave room for people to make their own sense of it. Never tell them, in words or images, what they are supposed to discover.

02

Invite, never persuade

Spark curiosity and leave room for doubt. No preaching, no dogma, no promises about what someone will feel.

03

Evidence without sterility

Make the science visible and legible while protecting warmth, poetry and first-person testimony.

04

Context is part of the product

Preparation, consent, facilitation and integration are not support content; they are core experience design.

05

Technology serves attention

Use VR only where immersion, embodiment or remote co-presence genuinely deepens the practice.

Recommendation / modality strategy

One aesthetic grammar. Six levels of access.

Glowacki’s 2024 paper suggests numadelic value lies in minimal representation, soft luminous boundaries and room for people’s own meaning, not in the headset alone. Each modality must test what it can genuinely preserve.

01 / SIGNATURE

Native social VR

Maximum attentional containment, transformation of embodiment and remote co-presence.

Best for
Flagship group and research experiences
Protect
Highest-fidelity interaction and facilitation
02 / BRIDGE

WebXR practice

A URL-delivered, install-light pathway on compatible headsets with hand input and spatial anchoring.

Best for
Trials, solo entry and rapid iteration
Protect
Capability fallbacks and measured fidelity
03 / EMERGING

6DoF spatial glasses

Lighter form factors may reduce the symbolic and physical weight of “putting on VR.”

Best for
Short practices woven into place
Protect
Do not design around unreleased hardware
04 / EVERYDAY AR

Face & body breath filters

Phone-camera fields respond to breath and movement, letting a person encounter a soft-boundary energy body without specialist hardware.

Best for
Discovery, breathing practice and social sharing
Protect
Avoid novelty filters that trivialise the practice
05 / TACTILE

Heart-worn light & haptics

A small chest-worn or hand-held object could translate breath or heart rhythm into restrained light and vibration during practice.

Best for
Eyes-closed practice and embodied continuity
Protect
Prototype as a non-medical device; test comfort and safety
06 / AMBIENT

Audio, projection & installation

Spatial audio, projected fields, light and facilitated ritual can widen access without claiming to reproduce VR.

Best for
Venues, retreats, public encounters and integration
Protect
Clear expectations about experiential depth
RECOMMENDED EXPERIMENT

Port one contained numadelic ritual, not the whole platform.

  1. Feasibility

    Choose one interaction; define frame-rate, thermal, asset and input budgets.

  2. Quest browser pilot

    Compare completion, comfort, presence and drop-off with the native build.

  3. Android XR adaptation

    Test the same capability layer on 6DoF glasses when production hardware is available.

  4. Fidelity decision

    Scale only if the practice preserves its intended conditions, not just technical compatibility.

Method and limits

How to read this analysis

01

Findings describe repeated or divergent response patterns. Closed-choice counts are calculated directly from the six exports.

02

Interpretations are reasoned hypotheses about what those patterns mean. They are deliberately labelled and should be challenged.

03

Recommendations apply strategic judgment. They are not automatically produced by majority vote.

04

This is a small internal sample, not market research. Qualitative strength reflects recurrence, specificity and relevance, not statistical significance.

05

Imported strategist answers stay separate from team counts so post-hoc influence does not rewrite the original evidence.
