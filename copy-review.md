# aNUma Strategy Observatory: copy v3, in Jose's voice

*22 Sept 2026. The page copy, rewritten from your six answers. Applied in `analysis-refined.html` (plus `analysis-refined.js` and `market-research-refined.js`). The original files are untouched.*

## How I wrote it

- **I and you.** You ran the fieldwork, so the page speaks as you: "I asked", "my read", "what I'd do". The team is "you". "We" only shows up where you really are in it together (the brand, the decisions).
- **Their words, with their names.** Gregory's "Hardware." and "VR that doesn't suck", Valerie's "knowing but not feeling" and her apology for naming more than one audience, Catherine's "there are quite a few of us", David's app-store comparison, Joe's Clear Light participants and "no code". People trust a synthesis when they recognise themselves in it.
- **Say the hard thing kindly.** "Big vision, no first customer." "The same confusion in nicer colours." "I won't pretend it can." Direct, never harsh.
- **Admit what's a guess.** "That's my judgment, not an algorithm, so push back." "Something to test, not something to trust."
- **Plain words over strategy words.** No doorway, legible, choreograph, materially, endogenous, container, journey. Contractions everywhere. Mixed sentence lengths, the odd fragment.
- **Fewer machine tells.** No em dashes, far fewer "X, not Y" constructions, and far fewer lists of three.
- **Labels too.** Buttons and kickers were part of the robotic feel: "Trace responses" is now "See who said what", "Reveal layer" is "Show more", "Boardroom synthesis" is "The honest read".

## What stayed

Facts and figures (Meta, Apple, XREAL, WebXR evidence lines, source titles), the counts and charts, the central line about meaning vs. translation and delivery, "Design conditions, not conclusions.", "The brand must behave before it decorates", "Four customers. Four reasons to pay.", "A clear sentence before a clever slogan", and the closing line. They already sounded like a person.

## Before you present

- If "People who try it get it" feels too strong for Catherine's caution, try "Most people who try it get it."
- Team names appear throughout. Strip them if the page ever goes beyond the six.
